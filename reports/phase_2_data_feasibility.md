# CreditAI Phase 2 — Real-Data Feasibility

**Scope:** Inventory of the files already under `Data/`, their supported uses, and the evidence needed before any real-data pilot. This report preserves the Phase 1 and Phase 1.1/1.2 results as prior prototype work; those synthetic results do not validate credit-risk prediction for Indian borrowers.

## Finding

There is no identified Indian borrower-level dataset in this workspace containing both point-in-time applicant/transaction features and subsequent repayment/default outcomes. The local UCI Taiwan and LendingClub U.S. datasets have borrower/loan-level targets and can support offline benchmark exercises under their stated limits. The German benchmark has a “good/bad risk” class, not a fully specified observed default horizon. Credit approval labels are decisions, not repayment outcomes. Synthetic outcomes remain synthetic. The available RBI and NPCI statistics are aggregates and cannot be used as borrower training examples.

## Data distinctions and gaps

1. **Borrower/loan outcome data:** UCI Taiwan and LendingClub U.S. contain borrower/loan-level outcomes; German Credit contains a proxy class. These permit historical benchmark experiments, but none establishes Indian-population performance. LendingClub status semantics and feature timestamps need auditing before any training.
2. **Aggregate context:** RBI and NPCI series describe sectors, districts, banks, or national payment activity. Aggregates cannot be assigned to individual applicants as labels and should not be joined to borrowers without a sound, authorized methodology.
3. **Synthetic data:** Every Phase 1-generated feature/transaction/outcome file is synthetic. It remains useful for deterministic plumbing and tests only. Synthetic outcomes must never be described as observed defaults.
4. **India-specific gap:** There is no verified India borrower-level dataset with authorized point-in-time features, repayment events, and enough follow-up to label defaults. CreditMitra narrations lack amounts, borrower keys, and outcomes and have no local authorization evidence.
5. **License/provenance gap:** UCI pages explicitly state CC BY 4.0 for the two named benchmarks. The non-UCI local dataset copies do not include usage terms. A filename or public download is not proof of a license or permission to redistribute personal or financial data.

## Recommended acquisition strategy

1. Begin with a lender/NBFC data partnership and a written data-use agreement covering purpose, lawful basis/consent where required, retention, access controls, deletion, audit, and permitted model use. Acquire only the minimum necessary pseudonymized application-time features and subsequent repayment events. Do not request credentials or integrate financial accounts in this phase.
2. Agree the target before ingestion: product-specific default definition (for example, lender-defined days-past-due/write-off), observation cutoff, outcome horizon, treatment of restructures/prepayments/cures, and exclusions. Obtain origination date, event dates, censoring/closure status, and enough mature follow-up.
3. Obtain a data dictionary, provenance, license/contract, extraction timestamp, schema/version, missingness semantics, and a stable pseudonymous join key. Keep direct identifiers out of the modeling dataset and separate access to any re-identification key.
4. Start with a secure offline feasibility sample, access review, and data-quality report. No raw records should be added to this repository. Use controlled storage and least privilege. Confirm applicable Indian privacy, lending, and credit-reporting obligations with the partner's legal/compliance teams; this report makes no compliance claim.
5. Use the existing UCI and LendingClub data only for benchmark methodology; retain aggregate data for context, and synthetic data for tests. Do not claim transferability from those results to Indian borrowers.

## Validation gates before a real-data pilot

- **Observed outcomes:** Verified actual repayment records and a lender-approved default definition, with label provenance and enough positive/negative cases. Approval/rejection decisions are not substitutes.
- **Windows:** Declare point-in-time observation window and outcome window, plus maturity/censoring rules, before feature/model work.
- **Leakage controls:** Enforce feature availability timestamps at scoring cutoff; exclude post-origination/post-default events, collections actions, downstream decision fields, future aggregates, duplicate borrower leakage, and repeat-loan leakage across splits as appropriate.
- **Evaluation:** Use a strictly later time-based holdout, ideally with development, validation, and final untouched test periods. Compare ROC-AUC, PR-AUC (against default prevalence), calibration curve, Brier score, and prevalence/constant-score and current-policy baselines. Report confidence intervals and subgroup sample sizes.
- **Thresholds:** Select decision thresholds from lender-defined false-positive/false-negative costs, capacity, policy constraints, and human review—not a generic 0.50 cutoff. Calibrate only on a permitted validation set; do not tune on final test.
- **Explainability and stability:** Assess global/local explanations, feature validity, subgroup error and calibration, temporal drift, missingness drift, sensitivity to source changes, and monitoring/retraining triggers.
- **Limitations and governance:** Document intended population/product, out-of-scope uses, uncertainty, fairness limitations, data provenance/rights, human oversight, access/retention, incident handling, and independent review before any consequential pilot.
